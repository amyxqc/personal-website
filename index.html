Why I do this

I came to planning from art and architecture, drawing buildings, public realms and people. Living in Hangzhou, Auckland, New York and Cambridge made me curious about urban change. Progress doesn’t happen by itself: we need to nurture the conditions for it. I studied economics and mathematical probability at Columbia to find those conditions. What is the impact of transit-oriented development? How does upzoning work, and who does it benefit? Why is it so hard to build public transit? What problems and inconveniences can intelligent market design solve, and when are market solutions not enough to reach the social optimum?

Now I want to turn research into the real world, using my planning expertise, cross-cultural and cross-disciplinary skills, relationships and beliefs to pull the levers that make change happen. I am particularly passionate about urban and development economics, real estate finance, environmental economics, market design and political economy. There is a lot of innovation and economic gain we can bring to city halls and industries by understanding problems deeply, thinking outside the box and being ambitious about the impact we can have. But with the proliferation of tools, there is often a loss of purpose: what is the technology built and designed for? I am training as an urban planner to better answer this question, in government and in how we plan and invest in infrastructure.

Flood Smart Assistant: AI to broaden human judgment and accelerate action

Flood decisions bring together competing definitions of risk and stakeholders who need different evidence. I’m fine-tuning the platform as a decision-support tool: AI should help teams see more, understand trade-offs and move from evidence to action, while people retain control over priorities and decisions.

Why it mattersExposure, economic loss, social vulnerability and recovery capacity can point to different investment priorities. The framework makes those differences visible instead of hiding them in one score.
Designed around stakeholdersCommunity partners, planners, analysts and decision-makers need different views of the evidence, from local impacts and scenario comparisons to methods, sources and resource trade-offs.
Human-in-the-loop AI · 4 agents + coordinatorI’m fine-tuning four specialist AI agents and one coordinator. Together they support evidence retrieval and checks, deterministic geospatial analysis and clear explanations of scenario trade-offs. People review recommendations, set priorities and decide what happens next.
My roleTeam lead and corresponding author. I led the method, the interface and the product framing, ran the sprints, organized expert reviews, and presented and demoed the work.

Representation for Planners · Harvard GSD · Oct. 2026 · Individual

City Planning at the Hinge of History

A story in seven drawings that shows my range of representation as an urban planner and designer. Lower Allston gave me a place-specific way to ask a larger question: to what extent should technological progress, including frontier AI, advance before safety and alignment are secured? The drawings study the district as Harvard’s expansion and the I-90 project reshape it, from the energy grid down to the street. They end on Lower Allston as a test case for balancing the speed and scale of frontier research with safety, social impact, collaboration and the public interest.

My roleIndividual work. I wrote the argument, made every map and drawing, and designed the layout.
ToolsRhino, Illustrator, InDesign, GIS
What it showsMoving across scales, from who supplies a neighborhood's power to the width of a bike lane, and drawing to persuade as well as to inform.

What I value

I believe we should measure success by tangible and tractable improvements in safety, climate resilience, and economic outcomes, rather than by the complexity of methods.

We need to begin inquiries with first-principled and high-leverage questions and critically examine arguments and counterarguments for each inquiry. Rigor is a responsibility.

While planners need to lead with the courage to tell the truth, I am aware that multiple truths can exist and we need to work together to find a solution. Pragmatism and idealism can co-exist.

I want humanity to flourish. And so, we need to ensure that institutions and AI tools remain transparent, safe, and aligned with human values. We need to answer to privacy, agency, and duty to others.
